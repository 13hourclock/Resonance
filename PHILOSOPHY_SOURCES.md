## 1. Om / Aum

### 1.1 What the sources say
1. **The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad** is a short Hindu scripture, twelve verses long, devoted entirely to Om.
   1. Verse 1: "Om! — This syllable is this whole world … the past, the present, the future — everything is just the word Om."
   2. Verses 8–12 read the syllable as A–U–M plus a fourth:
      1. **A** = the waking state (vaiśvānara);
      2. **U** = the dream state (taijasa);
      3. **M** = deep sleep (prājña);
      4. **the fourth (turīya)** is "without an element", beyond speech, the silence after the sound.
   - Sources:
     1. Translation in *A Source Book in Indian Philosophy*, ed. S. Radhakrishnan & C. A. Moore (Princeton, 1957), on Columbia University's teaching site: http://ftp.columbia.edu/itc/mealac/pritchett/00indciv/04upan/txt_mandukya_upan.html
     2. A scholarly overview: "Understanding Om (Aum), Turīya, and the Three States of Consciousness in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad", *Psychological Perspectives* (2008), doi:10.1080/00332920802457760.
2. **Om is sacred in several living religions:** Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism. It is chanted, written as the ॐ symbol and used to open and close prayers and recitations.
   - Source: *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, "Om": https://kids.britannica.com/scholars/article/om/57068
3. **Ik Onkar (ੴ)**, "One Creator / One Being", opens the Mūl Mantar, the first line of the Sikh scripture, the Guru Granth Sahib. It is a related but distinct Sikh concept and is not "the same as Om".
   - Sources:
     1. BBC Bitesize, Sikhism, Mool Mantar: https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/zk3f3k7/revision/1
     2. Oxford Reference, "Mūl Mantar".

### 1.2 Respect notes
1. Om is a **living sacred word and symbol**, not a historical curiosity. Never use the ॐ glyph, Ik Onkar (ੴ), a chanted or sung "Aum" or mantra recordings in the game, its icon or its marketing.

## 2. Nāda Brahma ("sound is the absolute")

### 2.1 What the sources say
1. In Indian thought, **nāda** (sound) is treated as a form of the absolute (brahman). Sources distinguish:
   1. **āhata**, "struck" sound: any audible sound made by contact;
   2. **anāhata**, "unstruck" sound: an inner, inaudible sound.

   This links sound, music and the sacred in Hindu philosophy and in Indian music theory.
   - Sources:
     1. Guy L. Beck, *Sonic Theology: Hinduism and Sacred Sound* (University of South Carolina Press, 1993): https://uscpress.com/Sonic-Theology. Also on Project MUSE.
     2. "Theology of Music and Hindu Religion", *Religions* 12(8):663 (2021), doi:10.3390/rel12080663.

## 3. The music of the spheres (Pythagoras, Boethius, Kepler)

### 3.1 Pythagoras and the Pythagoreans
1. **The ratios.** Early Pythagoreans linked the main consonances to simple whole-number ratios:
   1. octave 2:1;
   2. fifth 3:2;
   3. fourth 4:3.

   They saw number as underlying cosmic order (the tetraktys, 1+2+3+4).
2. **"Music of the heavens".** Later writers called it the "harmony of the spheres". The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* notes it is doubtful that Pythagoras himself thought in terms of spheres.
3. **The anvil and monochord stories** about Pythagoras discovering the ratios are later legends. The anvil version is physically wrong.
- Sources:
  1. SEP, "Pythagoras": https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pythagoras/
  2. SEP, "History of Western Philosophy of Music: Antiquity to 1800": https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hist-westphilmusic-to-1800/

### 3.2 Boethius
1. *De institutione musica* I.2 (early 6th c.) names three kinds of music:
   1. **musica mundana**: the music of the world, the heavens and the seasons. It is **inaudible**.
   2. **musica humana**: the harmony of body and soul.
   3. **musica instrumentalis**: audible music.
   - Source: Latin text in G. Friedlein (ed.), Teubner, Leipzig 1867, via the Thesaurus Musicarum Latinarum: https://chmtl.indiana.edu/tml/6th-8th/BOEMUS1C_MCTC944.html

### 3.3 Kepler
1. **The harmonies.** In *Harmonices Mundi* (Linz, 1619), Book V, Johannes Kepler compared the ratio of each planet's fastest and slowest angular speeds to musical intervals. He gave Earth's as mi–fa, about 16:15, a semitone.
2. **The third law.** In the same book he stated what is now his **third law**: the square of the orbital period ∝ the cube of the orbit size. A real scientific result came out of a musical-cosmic search.
- Sources:
  1. Smithsonian Libraries digital copy: https://library.si.edu/digital-library/book/ioanniskepplerih00kepl
  2. Bruce Stephenson, *The Music of the Heavens: Kepler's Harmonic Astronomy* (Princeton University Press, 1994).

### 3.4 Respect and accuracy notes
1. This is **historical philosophy**, not physics. Planets do not literally sing. The game should never say or imply they do.
2. Use Kepler's **harmonics**, not his astrology.
3. The tradition later fed into Neoplatonic, Christian, Kabbalistic and Sufi writings. None of those are used. If any are added, don't depict their practices or sacred names.

### 3.5 How it maps to the game
1. "Each layer modulates the next", the nested pulse and planets as ratios of the heartbeat (GOAL, WILBOW_VERBATIM.md:1349–1350; ratios PROPOSAL, SCALE_MUSIC §1). This is a playable "harmony of the spheres": the system "chord" and the galaxy "swell" (PROPOSAL milestones).

## 4. Cymatics: seeing vibration

### 4.1 What the sources say (real physics)
1. **Chladni figures.** Ernst Chladni, *Entdeckungen über die Theorie des Klanges* (Leipzig, 1787), bowed sand-covered plates. The sand collects along the **nodal lines** (places that don't move) of the plate's standing waves.
   - Source: Deutsches Textarchiv: https://www.deutschestextarchiv.de/book/show/chladni_klang_1787
2. **What sets the pattern.** It depends on the plate's shape, material, thickness, where it is held and the frequency. Higher modes give more complex patterns.
   - Sources:
     1. UNSW Physics (Joe Wolfe), "Chladni patterns": https://newt.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/chladni.html
     2. N. H. Fletcher & T. D. Rossing, *The Physics of Musical Instruments* (Springer, 1991).
3. **Hans Jenny** named the field "cymatics": *Kymatik / Cymatics: The Structure and Dynamics of Waves and Vibrations* (Basilius Presse, Basel, 1967). He photographed vibrating powders, pastes and liquids.

### 4.2 Pseudoscience warning
1. Online cymatics content often claims the patterns **prove** that sound creates matter or consciousness, that particular frequencies "heal", or that a sacred word makes a sacred shape. **These claims are not supported.**
2. A Chladni pattern is fully explained by plate mechanics. The same frequency gives a different pattern on a different plate.
3. Do not use "Om makes a perfect mandala" or similar viral claims.

## 5. The harmonic series, with Music Theory's accuracy points

These points come from Resonance Music Theory's input (relayed Oct 24 2026 in /workspace/philo_inputs/lore_and_theory_inputs.md, line 16 and the accuracy list). I checked them against the physics sources below and recalculated the cent values myself.

### 5.1 Accurate statements
1. **Partials.** A periodic tone (a sung or bowed note, a wind instrument) has partials at whole-number multiples of its lowest frequency: f, 2f, 3f… These are the **harmonic series**.
   - Source: UNSW Physics (Joe Wolfe), "Harmonics": https://newt.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/harmonics.html
2. **The simple ratios** come from neighbouring harmonics:
   1. 2:1 octave;
   2. 3:2 fifth;
   3. 4:3 fourth;
   4. 5:4 major third.
3. **Equal temperament vs the series** (12-TET, the standard Western tuning). Cents are 1/100 of a 12-TET semitone; my calculations, 1200·log2 of the ratio:

   | Harmonic | Cents (folded into one octave) | Nearest 12-TET note | Difference |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 3 | 702.0 | fifth (700) | 12-TET fifth is ~2 cents flat of pure |
   | 5 | 386.3 | major third (400) | 12-TET third is ~14 cents sharp of pure |
   | 7 | 968.8 | minor seventh (1000) | harmonic is ~31 cents flat |
   | 11 | 551.3 | between fourth (500) and tritone (600) | ~a quarter tone off either |
   | 13 | 840.5 | between minor and major sixth | ~40 cents off |
4. **The 7th and 11th harmonics are not the source of other tunings.** They *resemble* some maqam quarter-tone steps and blues "blue notes". Those traditions **do not derive** from the harmonic series. Don't say they do.
5. **Gamelan does not fit the series.**
   1. Gamelan instruments are mostly struck metal bars and gongs. Their partials are **inharmonic**.
   2. Sléndro and pélog tunings **vary from ensemble to ensemble**. Each set is tuned to itself.
   3. Never use gamelan as an example of "harmonic series" tuning.
6. **Struck sounds are inharmonic.** That includes bars, bells, drums and stones.
   - The old PROPOSAL "the first sound's strongest partial is the fundamental" (Q266) is withdrawn: the stick strike is LORE ONLY (2:44–2:45 AM): not a mechanic, and the om tone stands on his Q264 answer, not on the strike.
   - Source: UNSW, "How harmonic are harmonics?": https://newt.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/harmonics.html
7. **Plucked strings with special bridges**, such as the tanpura's grazing *jawārī* bridge, make unusually strong and buzzy overtones. That is why a tanpura drone sounds so rich.
   - Source: C. V. Raman, "On some Indian stringed instruments", *Proc. Indian Assoc. Cultivation of Science* 7:29–33 (1921): https://repository.ias.ac.in/69870/1/69870.pdf
8. **Overtone singing** shapes the mouth so single harmonics of a drone are heard as a melody. Mongolian *khöömei* is on UNESCO's Representative List (Mongolia 2010; China, "Mongolian art of singing: Khoomei", 2009). Tuvan *khöömei* is also a living tradition. UNESCO notes ritual and ceremonial uses.
   - Sources:
     1. https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/mongolian-traditional-art-of-khoomei-00396
     2. https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/mongolian-art-of-singing-khoomei-00210
9. **"Forty octaves up is light"** is a **metaphor**. Doubling an audio frequency about 40 times lands near visible-light frequencies numerically, but sound and light are different physical things. Use it only as a poetic idea (Q274).
10. **Music of the spheres and nāda brahma** are historical philosophy: inspiration, not science (§2, §3).

### 5.2 "Special" frequencies: conventions, not physics
1. **136.1 Hz "Om" / "Earth-year tone".**
   1. It comes from Hans Cousto, *The Cosmic Octave: Origin of Harmony* (English ed. Life Rhythm; German original 1984). He took the tropical year and doubled its frequency 32 times: 1/31,556,926 s × 2³² ≈ 136.10 Hz.
   2. Cousto's own material links it to chakras and "soothing" effects. **This is a new-age convention, not physics, and makes health claims we must not repeat.**
   3. Source: Cousto's site, https://www.planetware.de/octave/
2. **432 Hz.**
   1. Claims that A = 432 Hz is "natural" or healing are not supported by evidence.
   2. A 33-person pilot study (Calamassi & Pomponi, *Explore* 15(4):283–290, 2019, doi:10.1016/j.explore.2019.04.001, with a 2020 corrigendum) is often cited. It is small and preliminary, and its authors call for larger trials.
   3. Reuters Fact Check (2021) found the viral 432/440 Hz claims unfounded: https://www.reuters.com/article/fact-check/debunking-social-media-claims-about-a432hz-and-a440hz-musical-pitches-idUSL1N2P915O/
3. **528 Hz** ("DNA repair", "love frequency"). I found no credible evidence. Treat it like 432 Hz: do not use it, and never make claims.
4. **Note on the "~-32 cents" figure.**
   1. Music Theory's relayed input says a 64 Hz om "needs ~-32 cent global retune".
   2. Recalculated: 64 Hz is 37.6 cents below C2 (−37.6 cents): 1200·log2(65.406 / 64) = 37.6, matching SCALE_MUSIC.md §7.1.
   3. −31.4 cents is the A = 432.1 Hz / 136.1 Hz offset from A = 440, (1200·log2(432.1/440) = −31.4, my calculation). The two numbers were probably mixed up. Q264 should use 37.6 for 64 Hz.
